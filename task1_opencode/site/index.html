Our team take on AI

Copilot,
not captain.

Our shared working thesis: let AI do the repetitive bits, but keep people responsible for the meaning, the consequences, and the “wait, that can’t be right” moment.

“AI can write the query, but it still can't tell you when the dashboard is lying to you.”

— Bhavesh, and a useful team-sized reminder
01

Automate the boring. Keep the thinking.

AI is already great at boilerplate, routine data wrangling, and getting a first draft moving. That buys engineers more time for framing the right problem.

02

Generated is not the same as correct.

Code can compile and still be confidently wrong. People need to check the data, assumptions, context, and whether the output makes sense in the real world.

03

The future job title: professional judgment.

In 5–10 years, more routine coding may be automated. The valuable work will lean harder on problem framing, validation, system design, and communicating what the results mean.

End of page · beginning of sprint

Good software is a team sport.

We’re learning to build the future with tools that are clever, fallible, and still unable to find the missing semicolon by vibes alone.
